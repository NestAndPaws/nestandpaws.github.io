Floor Patterns: Herringbone, Chevron, Straight and Wide Plank

Same walls, door and window. Only the floor pattern changes. Compare four on screen, then plan the layout on your own floor.

By Nest & Paws · Checked Nov 19, 2026 · How I pick

A pattern name on a sample card is hard to picture across a whole room. This guide shows one hall with the same walls, six-panel door, window and baseboards over four floor patterns, so the layout is the only thing that changes.

The images are AI-generated concept pictures, not photos of a real floor. The board sizes and the wood tone are for comparison only, not a product. A real floor depends on the board size, the room shape and how your installer lays it out.

Four floor patterns, one hall

Straight plank and wide plank run in one direction. Herringbone zigzags with square-ended blocks. Chevron meets angled planks in a continuous V. Walls, door, window and baseboards stay the same in every look.

Before you pick a pattern

Start with the room

Patterns draw the eye. In a large open room a pattern can be the feature. In a small or busy room, straight or wide plank tends to feel calmer. Picture the rugs and furniture that will cover part of the floor.

Pick the direction

With straight boards, installers often suggest running them along the longest wall or toward the main light. Ask yours which suits your room, and ask how the floor will meet the next room at the doorway.

Herringbone or chevron

Herringbone uses square-ended blocks laid at right angles, so the zigzag steps at each block. Chevron cuts the board ends at an angle so the V lines run unbroken. Chevron tends to look sharper, and herringbone tends to look more textured.

Ask about the layout work

A pattern usually takes more cutting and fitting than straight boards. Ask your installer about extra material and extra labor before you compare quotes, and ask how the pattern will end at walls and doorways.

Check the subfloor

A pattern can show uneven spots and gaps more than straight boards do. Ask your installer to check that the subfloor is flat, and to explain where the layout will start.

Check the product and the wood

Not every product comes in every pattern, and chevron is often sold as matched left and right pieces. Wider boards can move more with humidity. Read the maker's install guide, and ask whether solid or engineered wood suits your room.

If you do one thing

If you do one thing, ask to see a few boards laid out in the pattern, and look at them from the doorway in your own light. A pattern that looks calm on a screen can feel busy across a real room.

What I left out

  • Flooring, adhesive and tool products, since the right ones depend on your subfloor and the maker's directions.
  • Cost and install time, since quotes depend on the room, the pattern and your installer.

  • The four patterns cover two straight layouts, one with narrow boards and one with wide boards, plus two popular zigzag layouts, so you can compare very different directions on one floor.
