Entryway Storage Ideas: 5 Setups in One Small Entry

Same door, window, walls and floor. Only the storage changes. Compare five setups, then match one to what you carry in the door.

By Nest & Paws · Checked Nov 19, 2026 · How I pick

A storage idea is hard to judge in a small entry. This guide shows one entry with the same door, window, walls and floor and five different storage setups, so the storage is the only thing that changes.

The images are AI-generated concept pictures, not photos of a real entry, and the pieces shown are setup ideas rather than specific products. Most of these setups sit on the floor or hang from a few hooks, so you can try one without a remodel.

Five setups, one small entry

Each setup adds one main storage piece and a few small items. The door, window, walls and floor stay the same.

Before you pick a setup

Start with what you carry in

Houzz organizers suggest the entry hold only one or two seasonal jackets or coats per family member, daily bags, and one or two pairs of shoes per person. Count those first, then pick the setup that holds that much and no more.

Use the wall before the floor

Wayfair and House Digest both point to wall hooks and a wall shelf as storage that takes no floor space. The Wall hooks setup shows this: a peg rail, a shelf above and a small boot tray are the only things that touch the floor.

Keep a clear path

LivingEtc says designers suggest at least 36 inches of clearance for people to walk through. Measure the walkway before you buy, and check that a cabinet door or the front door can still swing open.

Give shoes a real home

A bench with a shelf underneath, a closed cabinet and an open shelf all hold shoes in different ways. Closed doors hide the mess but need room to open. An open shelf is quick to use but shows every pair, so it works best for one or two pairs per person.

Add a place for small things

West Elm suggests a key rack or mail tray so small items stop going missing, and shelves above hooks for bins, gloves and hats. The Console with bins setup uses a key rack and a key tray for that job.

Check the mounting if you rent

ForRent suggests asking your property manager before you install a shelf with hooks. AOL notes that no-drill adhesive shelves may not hold as much weight, so keep them for light items like keys.

Watch the downsides

A bench with baskets can turn into a pile if you stuff it. Hooks fill up fast, and Consumer Reports notes swivel hooks can keep one person's items to a set spot. A cabinet or console blocks more of the walkway than a wall rack.

If you do one thing

If you do one thing, put hooks on the wall by the door and a tray for keys. They need almost no floor space, and you can add a bench or shelf later if shoes still pile up.
