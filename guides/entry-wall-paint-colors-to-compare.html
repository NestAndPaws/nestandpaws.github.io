Entryway Paint Colors: 5 Wall Colors in One Entry

Same door, window, console and floor. Only the wall color changes. Compare five on screen, then sample the two you like on your own wall.

By Nest & Paws · Checked Nov 18, 2026 · How I pick

A paint chip is small, and an entry wall is the first one you see. This guide shows one entryway with the same white six-panel door, window, oak console, round mirror and plank floor in five Sherwin-Williams wall colors, so the wall is the only thing that changes.

The images are AI-generated concept pictures, not photos of a real entryway. The code, hex value and light reflectance for each color come from Sherwin-Williams' own color pages, read Oct 25 and Nov 18, 2026. A screen only shows a closest match, never the paint itself, so test a sample on your own wall before you buy a can.

Five wall colors, one entry

Light reflectance (LRV) runs from 0 to 100, and lower numbers mean a darker color. The five run from Naval at 4 to Alabaster at 82. The ceiling stays warm white in every look and the door and trim keep their original color.

Before you paint the entry

Sample on the wall, not on a chip

Paint a patch about two feet square on your own entry wall and look at it in the morning, at midday and at night under your own bulb. An entry often has little daylight, so the color can shift a lot.

Check it next to your trim and door

The concept images use warm white trim and a white door. If yours are bright white or a different tone, hold the sample next to them, since the same color can look warmer or cooler beside each.

Think about the floor

The floor in these images is a warm medium oak. Cool colors like Naval and Evergreen Fog read cooler against warm wood, and Cavern Clay reads warmer. Hold the sample near your own floor.

Match the paint to the wall

Entry walls take scuffs from bags and shoes. Check the can or the color page for the finish, and ask the store which sheen the maker suggests for a high-traffic wall.

Prep the wall

Wipe off dust and scuff marks, patch holes and let it dry before you paint. Check the can for the recoat time and the number of coats the maker suggests, since dark colors over a light wall can need more.

If you do one thing

If you do one thing, sample your top two colors on the wall itself and look at them at three times of day. A color that wins on a screen can lose in your own light.

What I left out

  • Paint, primer and brushes sold by the can, since the right product depends on your wall and the maker's directions.
  • Any color without a code and a reading on the maker's own page.
  • Product links, since this guide is about the color choice.

How I chose the colors

  • The five colors cover a warm white, a dark blue, a greige, a terracotta and a gray-green, so you can compare very different directions in one entry.
  • Codes, hex values and light reflectance were read from the Sherwin-Williams color pages on Oct 25 and Nov 18, 2026.
  • The wall is recolored on one image in code, so the door, window, console, mirror and floor stay the same in every look.
